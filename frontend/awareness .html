Three Golden Rules

These simple habits can help prevent common digital-payment and impersonation scams.

pause_circle

Pause Before You Act

Urgency is a common pressure tactic. Stop and verify independently before tapping a link, approving a transaction, or downloading an app.

verified

Verify Directly

Contact the organisation using its official website, app, card, or trusted phone number rather than replying to the suspicious message.

lock

Protect Your Secrets

Never share your UPI PIN, passwords, OTPs, or other authentication secrets with callers, merchants, or supposed support agents.

Common Scams to Recognize

Tap a card to see the warning signs

Warning signs

  • • Unexpected payment request
  • • Fake cashback or refund promise
  • • Request to enter your UPI PIN to “receive” money
  • • Pressure to act immediately

NPCI says the UPI PIN is used to authorize bank transactions and should not be shared.

Check these first

  • • Strange or misspelled domain
  • • Unexpected shortened URL
  • • Urgent account warning
  • • Request for OTP/password

Remember

India's National Cyber Crime Threat Analytics Unit says there is no concept of “digital arrest” under Indian law. Do not transfer money because someone threatens you with arrest over a video call.

Red flags

  • • Guaranteed or unrealistic returns
  • • Pressure to deposit more money
  • • Fake profit dashboards
  • • Withdrawal blocked until another fee is paid

Verify independently

End the conversation and contact the real organisation or person using a trusted number or official application.

Stop

Do not install unknown applications at someone's request. If access was already granted, disconnect, remove suspicious permissions, secure important accounts, and seek trusted technical help.
